## 30-Second Positioning

I am an Electrical Engineer in automotive electronics who uses Codex primarily to build engineering workflow tools. The tools help structure work around requirements, WCCA preparation, validation planning, design-review readiness, and documentation. The focus is practical: reusable workflows, sanitized inputs, traceable outputs, and human engineering review.

## Two-Minute Positioning

A lot of AI use in engineering stalls because it remains a pile of one-off prompts. My approach is to identify repeated engineering pain points, build a small Codex-assisted tool or workflow, validate the output, document the process, and make it reusable. Public examples use synthetic automotive lighting data, but the workflows map to real engineering tasks: requirements traceability, WCCA prep, risk/assumption tracking, and review readiness.

## STAR Story 1 — Requirements Traceability

**Situation:** Engineering inputs can arrive scattered across notes, tables, and review comments.

**Task:** Create a public-safe demo showing how AI can structure messy inputs without making final engineering decisions.

**Action:** Built a synthetic workflow that extracts candidate requirements, assigns IDs, proposes verification methods, flags ambiguity, and exports a trace matrix. Codex supports code, tests, and docs; the engineer reviews every classification.

**Result:** The artifact demonstrates traceability, structured output, and human review discipline without exposing proprietary content.

## STAR Story 2 — WCCA Prep

**Situation:** WCCA preparation can be slowed by scattered assumptions, tolerance values, plots, and report formatting.

**Task:** Create an AI-assisted preparation workflow using synthetic LED-driver data.

**Action:** Used Codex-assisted planning to scaffold parameter tables, missing-data checks, corner-case calculations, plots, and a review memo.

**Result:** The artifact shows how AI can reduce setup burden while keeping equations, assumptions, and conclusions under engineer review.

## STAR Story 3 — Codex Tool Building

**Situation:** AI proficiency can look vague unless it produces working tools.

**Task:** Show a controlled development process from engineering problem to tested utility.

**Action:** Defined SPEC.md, TASKS.md, AGENTS.md, synthetic inputs, code modules, tests, outputs, and README documentation.

**Result:** The case study proves repeatable tool-building, not generic prompting.

## Likely Interview Questions

### Are these real work examples?

The workflows are based on real engineering pain points, but the public artifacts use synthetic or sanitized data. I do not expose customer, supplier, schematic, BOM, harness, requirement, validation, or internal program information.

### Does AI make engineering decisions?

No. AI helps organize inputs, scaffold scripts, generate draft outputs, and flag gaps. Final decisions, assumptions, calculations, and design approval remain with qualified engineers.

### What makes this different from normal prompting?

Repeatability. A prompt gives an answer. A workflow has inputs, outputs, schemas, tests, review gates, and documentation that another engineer can use.

### Where does Codex fit?

Codex helps plan, implement, test, refactor, and document the workflow tools. Jose defines the engineering problem, reviews assumptions, and owns the final judgment.
